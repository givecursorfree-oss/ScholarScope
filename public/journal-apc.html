How to check a journal’s APC before you submit

Before you format a manuscript for a Q1-looking title, answer four questions with evidence: What is the APC? Is it open access — and under which license? Does the venue fit your field? Which free metrics and indexing signals can you verify?

Why APC checks matter

Article processing charges can range from zero to several thousand dollars. Labs and funders often need a clear fee before submission. Jumping between DOAJ, OpenAlex, SciMAGO, and publisher pages wastes time and makes it easy to miss a license or fee update.

A solid pre-submit check is not “find any impact number.” It is a short publishing brief you can share with a supervisor: fees, OA model, subjects, and links to verify ranks on official sites.

A practical checklist

  1. Identify the journal precisely — use ISSN (print or electronic) or the exact title. Fuzzy title matches can land on the wrong venue.
  2. Read the publisher page for listed fees — APC, page charges, color charges, or submission fees. Screenshot or note the date; fees change.
  3. Cross-check OA status in DOAJ — if the journal is indexed, confirm open access and Creative Commons license.
  4. Scan subjects and topics — DOAJ subjects and OpenAlex topics help you avoid misaligned venues.
  5. Use free impact metrics honestly — OpenAlex 2-year mean citedness, h-index, and i10 are useful signals. They are not Clarivate Journal Impact Factor.
  6. Verify SJR on SCImago — open the official SCImago journal page for SJR and quartile. Do not trust invented ranks in a third-party UI.
  7. Confirm indexing claims — DOAJ membership and MEDLINE/PubMed Catalog links (when available) are stronger than vague “indexed in…” marketing copy.

Trusted open sources (and what each is for)

  • DOAJ — open access status, licenses, and many declared APCs for OA journals.
  • OpenAlex — free citation-style metrics and topic metadata for millions of works and venues.
  • SCImago Journal & Country Rank — official place to confirm SJR and related ranking views.
  • Publisher journal pages — first place to confirm the fee you will actually be asked to pay.
  • NLM Catalog / PubMed — useful for biomedical indexing verification.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating any “impact factor” badge as Clarivate JIF without a source
  • Trusting APC numbers in a browser extension without checking the publisher
  • Choosing a journal by quartile alone while ignoring APC and license
  • Assuming DOAJ listing means “no APC” — many OA journals still charge
  • Submitting before your funder’s OA policy and license requirements are checked

FAQ

What is a journal APC?

An article processing charge is a fee some journals charge authors to publish, especially for open access. Always confirm the current amount on the publisher’s site before you pay.

Where should I look first for APC and OA data?

Publisher page first, then DOAJ for OA journals, then OpenAlex for additional metadata. ScholarScope follows that same preference order.

Are OpenAlex metrics the same as Clarivate Impact Factor?

No. OpenAlex citedness, h-index, and i10 are free indicators. For SJR, open the official SCImago page. ScholarScope never invents Clarivate IF.
